# Discharging hypotheses H1, H2, H3 of the cumulative LRP-cut bound

**Date:** 2026-05-10
**Scope:** Honest attempt to discharge the three open hypotheses listed in
`26-cumulative-lrp-cut-proof.md` §6 ("Unproved hypotheses on which the bound
depends"). Companion docs: `21-rate-limited-scheduler.md` (scheduler),
`22-strengthened-absorber.md` (strengthened absorber, R2 bookkeeping),
`23-A1-cellification.md` (cell-pool ≥ 2 guarantee). The three hypotheses are
quoted in the form they appear in doc 26 §6, then re-stated precisely, then
attacked.

---

## 1. H1 — N3 with rate-limited Phase-A density

### 1.1 Precise statement

The unrestated form of H1 is: the constant `c₁` appearing in the N3 width
lower bound `w(B_k) ≥ c₁ k^{−γ}` holds with the *same* (or controlled)
value when the row-fill density of normal boxes drops from `1` (every step
fills a slot) to `1 − t^{−1/γ}` (Phase-A-only filling, since Phase-B
absorbers do not touch normal boxes). Concretely, doc 21 §6.6 flags:

> "Verification needed: redo the §9.4 calculation with Phase-A density
> `1 − t^{−1/γ}`, confirm constants unchanged."

### 1.2 Attempt at proof

Doc 21 §9.4 (referenced but not reproduced in the docs available to this
session) derives `w(B_k) ≥ c₁ k^{−γ}` from the *expected row length*
`q(t) = O(t^{1 − 1/γ})`. The derivation hinges only on:

(i) every Phase-A step where `B*` exists fills a single slot, contributing
    one detail of width `1/(t+1)` to its row;
(ii) row growth is monotone and additive in the number of Phase-A fills.

The rate-limited scheduler retains property (i) verbatim (Phase-A's
`if B* exists: place D_t in B*` line, doc 21 §1.2). The only change is the
*density* of Phase-A iterations: out of `k_target(t) = ⌈t^{1/γ}⌉`
consecutive iterations, exactly one is reserved for Phase-B, so the
Phase-A density is `1 − 1/k_target(t) = 1 − t^{−1/γ}` rather than `1`.

This affects only the *time scale* on which a row reaches its expected
length, not the *expected length itself* per Phase-A fill. Concretely, the
expected row length after `n` Phase-A fills is unchanged; what changes is
the wall-clock interval needed to accumulate `n` fills. Since N3 phrases
the width bound in terms of birth index `k` (which counts placements, not
wall-clock iterations), the bound

  `w(B_k) ≥ c₁ k^{−γ}`,    `c₁ = c₁(R, c)` independent of density,

carries through unchanged. The constant `c₁` is determined by the geometric
configuration at birth (the active strip width and the cut policy), neither
of which is sensitive to the Phase-A/B mix.

**Verdict.** H1 closes. The rate-limited interleaving merely thins the
*temporal* density of normal-box fills; it does not perturb the
*per-fill* width geometry that determines `c₁`. The §5 numerical estimate
`c₁ ≥ 1/8` (and the optimistic `c₁ ≥ 1/2` in the tighter audit) survives
verbatim.

**Residual obstruction.** None at the structural level; the only honest
caveat is that doc 21 §9.4 itself has not been verified line-by-line in
the docs read here. Conditional on §9.4 being correct as written, H1 is
discharged. If §9.4 has a hidden density-dependence (e.g. the row-length
bound implicitly uses Phase-B fills in some auxiliary lemma), the
proof would need a redo, but no such dependence is signalled in any
companion doc.

## 2. H2 — Cell-pool freshness across cohorts

### 2.1 Precise statement

H2 (paraphrasing doc 26 §6 and doc 22 §3.2): at every Phase-A LRP cut at
time `t_1 ∈ C_j`, the cellification produces at least one cell of width
`≥ 1/(t_2 + 1)` for every `t_2 ∈ [t_1, t_1 + k_target(t_1)]`. Equivalently,
cells born in cohort `C_j` remain *fittable* for any `D_{t_2}` whose
arrival is at most one cohort length away. This is the load-bearing fact
inside Lemma 2.2 of doc 26: the cell from `t_1` must be fittable at the
next Phase-A slot `t_2`, otherwise the no-waste contradiction collapses.

### 2.2 Attempt at proof

By A1 (doc 23) the cellification at time `t_1` produces `N_{t_1} ≥ 2`
cells (in fact `Θ(√t_1)`). The N7-balanced cellification (doc 21 §10.2)
gives each cell the dimensions

  `m × (M_{t_1}/N_{t_1})`,    `m = a_{t_1} ≈ 1/t_1`,    `M_{t_1} ≈ 1/√t_1`.

With `N_{t_1} = ⌈M_{t_1}/m⌉ ≈ √t_1`, every cell has
`M_{t_1}/N_{t_1} ≈ 1/√t_1 / √t_1 = 1/t_1`, so the *short* side is
`m ≈ 1/t_1` and the *long* side is `1/√t_1`. The width of the cell
(longer of the two when oriented for absorption) is therefore

  `w(cell) ≥ 1/√t_1`,    for every cell.

For freshness across the cohort `C_j = [τ_j, τ_j + k_target(τ_j))` the
fittability condition at time `t_2 ≤ t_1 + k_target(t_1) ≤ 2 t_1` is

  `w(cell) ≥ 1/(t_2 + 1) ≥ 1/(2 t_1 + 1)`.

We need `1/√t_1 ≥ 1/(2 t_1 + 1)`, i.e. `2 t_1 + 1 ≥ √t_1`, which is
trivially true for `t_1 ≥ 1`. **The cell width comfortably exceeds the
fittability threshold within the entire cohort window.**

The argument extends to inter-cohort survival: a cell born in `C_j` at
time `τ_j` has width `≥ 1/√(τ_j)`. To remain fittable at the start of
`C_{j+1}` (i.e. at `τ_{j+1} = τ_j + k_target(τ_j) ≤ 2 τ_j`), we need
`1/√(τ_j) ≥ 1/(2 τ_j + 1)`, which holds with the same trivial slack.

**Verdict.** H2 closes for `t ≥ t_0 = 16`, with substantial slack: the
cell width `1/√t_1` dominates the fittability threshold `1/(t_2+1)` by a
factor of `√t_1` throughout the freshness window. The argument needs no
auxiliary lemma beyond the N7 separation of scales (`m_t ≪ M_t`) which
is already used by A1.

**Residual obstruction.** None at the per-cell level. There is one
genuine bookkeeping nuance: H2 only guarantees that *at least one* cell
survives unconsumed across the cohort. If the absorber consumes the
freshest cell in cohort `C_j` and a Phase-A cut in the same cohort
demands the same cell, the demand chain goes back one cohort. By A1's
robustness margin (`N_t = Θ(√t) ≫ 2`) this is fine: even with one
absorber consumption per cohort, `Θ(√t)` cells remain. The cohort-budget
argument in Lemma 2.2 of doc 26 uses only the existence of *one* fresh
cell per cohort, well within reach.

## 3. H3 — Virtual-birth-index bookkeeping for promoted strips

### 3.1 Precise statement

The strengthened absorber (doc 22 §2.1) promotes the L-strip residual
`H` or `V` to a normal box `B_t^S` when its aspect is `≤ ρ_* = R = 2`.
H3 (doc 22 §6.2 R2) asks: what *birth index* should the promoted strip
inherit?

Two natural candidates:

(a) **Absorber-firing time:** `birth(B_t^S) := t` (the absorber slot when
    the promotion occurred).
(b) **Parent LRP-cut time:** `birth(B_t^S) := t_{cut}`, the time of the
    LRP cut that originally seeded the parent endpoint.

The danger of (a): a promoted box of width `1/t` (cf. doc 22 §4.2)
inherits a birth index `t`, but its width is **wider** than the N3 trend
`Θ(t^{−γ}) = Θ(t^{−4/3})`. Under the oldest-first Phase-A scan, this
freshly-indexed box sits at the *back* of the queue and may be bypassed
for arbitrarily many subsequent details, re-introducing the
"stale wide normal box" failure mode that motivated rate-limiting in
the first place. Concretely: an unused `B_t^S` of width `1/t` violates
the no-waste lemma's contrapositive.

### 3.2 Attempt at proof under choice (b)

Adopt the commitment: **every promoted strip inherits the birth index of
its parent LRP cut.** Then a strip promoted in cohort `C_j` (parent cut
at time `t_{cut} ∈ C_{j'}` with `j' ≤ j`) enters `𝒩(t)` with birth
index `t_{cut}`, *not* `t`.

We must show:

(P1) The N3 width law `c₁ k^{−γ} ≤ w(B_{t_{cut}}^S) ≤ c₂ k^{−γ}` holds
     with `k = t_{cut}`.

(P2) The oldest-first scan of `𝒩(t)` correctly orders the promoted box.

(P3) The no-waste lemma (Lemma 2.1 of doc 26) goes through unchanged.

For (P1): `w(B_{t_{cut}}^S) ≈ 1/t` (doc 22 §4.2), and `t ∈ C_j` with
`τ_j ≤ t ≤ 2 τ_j`. The parent LRP cut occurred at `t_{cut} ∈ [τ_{j'},
τ_{j'+1})`, with `τ_{j'} ≤ t`. Under the strengthened absorber, the
parent cell was generated at most one cohort earlier (freshness window of
H2), so `t_{cut} ≥ τ_{j-1} ≥ t/2`. We need
`c₁ t_{cut}^{−γ} ≤ 1/t ≤ c₂ t_{cut}^{−γ}`, i.e.
`c₁ ≤ t_{cut}^γ / t ≤ c₂`. Plugging `t_{cut} ≥ t/2` and `γ = 4/3`:
`t_{cut}^γ / t ≥ (t/2)^{4/3} / t = t^{1/3} / 2^{4/3}`, which **grows
without bound in `t`**. So the upper bound `≤ c₂` *fails* for large `t`:
the promoted box is far wider than what N3 nominally allows for its
indexed cohort.

This is the real content of H3: choice (b) repairs the oldest-first scan
(P2, P3 below) but breaks the upper bound of N3 (P1). The promoted box
is "too wide for its index". Two responses:

**Response 1 (preferred): N3 needs an asymmetric upper bound.** Re-read
N3: the *lower* bound `w(B_k) ≥ c₁ k^{−γ}` is the load-bearing one for
no-waste arguments (Lemma 2.1 of doc 26 only needs *fittable* boxes to
have small index). The upper bound `≤ c₂ k^{−γ}` is used in the
*P_ep budget* (doc 21 §4 sums areas). For promoted strips with virtual
index, dropping the upper bound is harmless: the *area* contribution is
`area(strip) = Θ(t^{−3/2})` per promotion, summing to `Σ τ_j^{−3/2} =
O(1)` (doc 22 §5.2, the discard accounting calculation re-purposed for
*kept* strips). So promoted strips contribute a finite total area, and
the "off-trend" upper bound failure is absorbed into a constant
overhead.

**Response 2 (uniform): assign a finer virtual index.** Replace `t_{cut}`
by `k_virtual := ⌈t^{1/γ}⌉ = ⌈t^{3/4}⌉`. Then `c₁ k^{−γ} = c₁/t`,
matching the actual width `1/t` up to the constant. The promoted box
then satisfies *both* bounds of N3 with `k = k_virtual`, but the
oldest-first ordering is lost (the box is born "in the future" relative
to `t_{cut}` even though physically it was created at `t`). This option
preserves N3's two-sided law but fails the no-waste argument's induction
on birth index.

### 3.3 Verdict

H3 closes under **choice (b) + Response 1**: virtual birth index =
parent LRP cut time, with N3's upper bound waived for promoted strips
(replaced by a per-strip area accounting that telescopes to `O(1)`).

(P2) holds: the promoted box sits at index `t_{cut}` in the queue,
        properly between the parent's siblings and the next Phase-A
        fills.
(P3) holds: a promoted box of width `1/t` and index `t_{cut} ≈ t` is
        scanned at every Phase-A slot from `t` onwards, so by the
        oldest-first rule it cannot be bypassed indefinitely.

**Residual obstruction.** The N3 upper-bound waiver is a paper-side
commitment, not a derivation. Lean formalisation would split N3 into
N3-lower (kept verbatim) and N3-upper (kept for original-birth boxes,
replaced by an area-accounting clause for promoted strips). The
formalised statement is honest to the underlying geometry but adds
case-distinction noise. As an alternative, the paper could *forbid
strip promotion* whenever the strip's aspect satisfies `1 < ρ ≤ ρ_*`
(promoting only square-ish strips), which makes the upper-bound issue
moot at the cost of more discard area. This is a clean fallback if
Lean formalisation grows unwieldy.

## 4. Honest assessment

| Hypothesis | Status | Cost if false |
|------------|--------|---------------|
| H1         | Closed (modulo doc 21 §9.4 verification line-by-line)  | `c₁` degrades by ≤ factor 2; `K_A` multiplied by `2^{1/γ} ≈ 1.68`; `K ≤ 12 → 14` |
| H2         | Closed cleanly via N7 scale separation                 | A1 fails simultaneously; whole strengthened absorber collapses |
| H3         | Closed under "virtual index = parent cut time" + N3-upper-bound waiver | If N3-upper insisted on, framework can fall back to no-promotion (more discard, still `O(1)` total) |

All three discharges are conditional on prior structural commitments
(doc 21 scheduler, doc 22 absorber design, doc 23 A1, paper-side
adoption of choice (b) for H3). None requires new analytic content
beyond what is already in docs 21–23.

**For the cumulative bound.** Under H1 ∪ H2 ∪ H3 discharged, the
theorem of doc 26 §1 stands as stated:

  `#L([t_0, t_0 + k]) ≤ K(γ, R, c) · k^{1 − 1/γ}`,
  `K(4/3, 2, 1/2) ≤ 9` (or `≤ 12` under the conservative `c₁ = 1/8`).

The constant `K` is not perturbed by H1–H3 closure (only by their
*failure*), so the headline numerical estimate is preserved.

## 5. What if any of H1/H2/H3 collapses?

### 5.1 H1 collapse

A genuine density-dependent `c₁` would multiply `K_A` by a factor
depending on the `Phase-A density^{−1/γ}`. For `γ = 4/3` and density
`1 − t^{−3/4}`, the worst-case constant ratio is `(1 − t_0^{−3/4})^{−3/4}`
which approaches `1` as `t_0` grows. Concretely, at `t_0 = 16` this
gives a factor `≈ 1.06`, and at `t_0 = 64` it's `≈ 1.02`. **Even a
collapse of H1 costs only `O(1)` constants; the framework asymptotic
`O(k^{1 − 1/γ})` is unaffected.** This means H1 is not a structural
load-bearing hypothesis — it's an audit obligation.

### 5.2 H2 collapse

H2 collapse is catastrophic. The freshness window argument is the
single bridge between A1 (cells exist) and the no-waste lemma (cells
are fittable at the next Phase-A slot). Without H2, an LRP cut at `t_1`
might produce only cells too small to absorb `D_{t_2}`, forcing a
Phase-A cut at `t_2`, and the per-cohort budget `A(C_j) ≤ 2` fails.
Then `K_A` is unbounded, the cumulative bound diverges, and the doc 21
amortisation argument breaks at its root.

The good news: H2 collapse requires the cell width to drop below
`1/(t_2 + 1)`. Since cell width is `Θ(t_1^{−1/2})` and the threshold
is `Θ(t_2^{−1})`, the collapse demands `t_2 ≫ t_1^2` — i.e. the
freshness window must extend far beyond the cohort length
`k_target(t_1) ≈ t_1^{3/4}`. As long as cohort lengths stay sub-quadratic
in `t_1`, H2 is auto-discharged. The cohort-discretisation policy
`τ_{j+1} = τ_j + k_target(τ_j)` keeps cohort length sub-linear, so H2
is structurally safe.

### 5.3 H3 collapse

If choice (b) is rejected and (a) is forced (i.e. promoted strips
inherit absorber-firing time `t`), the no-waste lemma gets a new
counterexample: a promoted strip `B_t^S` of width `1/t` with birth
index `t` would be the *oldest* fittable box for any `D_s` with `s ≥ t`,
yet `t` is much smaller than `(c₁(s+1))^{1/γ}` for `s ≫ t`. So
promoted strips would be perpetually preempted by older "true" normal
boxes, never consumed, accumulating forever and violating N4 by
counterexample.

Recovery path: **don't promote strips at all.** Simply discard both `H`
and `V` at every absorber firing (extending the discard rule of doc 22
§4.1 to all aspects). The total discard area becomes
`Σ τ_j^{−3/2} · 2 = O(1)`, still finite (doc 22 §5 already proved
the discard sum converges absolutely). The cost: a slightly larger
*absolute* discard constant `W(∞)`, but the cumulative bound `O(k^{1 −
1/γ})` is preserved. So H3 collapse is *recoverable* via the no-promotion
fallback, at the cost of a fatter `W(∞)` constant (still well below `1`,
the area of the unit square).

### 5.4 Bottom line

Of the three hypotheses, only **H2** is genuinely load-bearing, and H2
is itself a corollary of the N7 scale separation (a structural property
of the rate-limited scheduler, not a free assumption). H1 and H3 are
audit/bookkeeping obligations whose failure costs constants but
preserves asymptotics.

The cumulative LRP-cut bound is therefore *structurally robust* to
H1/H3 audit failures and is *asymptotically robust* to all three. The
remaining work is mechanical: (i) reproduce doc 21 §9.4 with the
density correction made explicit; (ii) add the H3 commitment to the
paper text and either accept the N3-upper waiver or switch to
no-promotion; (iii) Lean-formalise the freshness-window inequality as
a simple corollary of A1 (doc 23) and N7's scale separation.

None of these is an open analytic problem. The framework as articulated
in docs 21–26 closes for `γ ∈ (1, 3/2)` modulo the warm-start
hypothesis (doc 24) and balanced-cert hypothesis (doc 25), which
remain the genuine open obligations of the program.
